\section{Performance of Forecasting Models}
The performance of the five decision tree-based ensemble models is assessed via MAPE, RMSE, and MAE metrics. \autoref{tab:performance} shows the scores for each model,

The large discrepancy between RMSE and MAE scores in terms of kWh suggests that the dataset contains many large outliers. Since RMSE squares the errors before averaging, large errors are penalized more, compared to MAE where all errors are treated equally via the absolute value.

Results indicate that the inclusion of external factors does help with improving the forecast accuracy in terms of MAPE, but that MAE and especially RMSE scores may actually worsen, a pattern that is apparent among all models. MAPE is more sensitive to errors on small consumption values, while RMSE is more sensitive to high consumption values and outliers. This suggests that the external factors help with increasing forecast accuracy for households with a lower and more stable consumption, while households with a high or varied consumption do not benefit from the external factors

A pattern occurring for all models is that on average, the models overestimate the electricity consumption during the winter months in the beginning of the year, as illustrated in \autoref{fig:avg-actual-vs-predicted}. During the summer, the average predictions lie closer to the actual average consumption of the households. The scatter plots in \autoref{fig:scatter-plots} show that the overestimation of electricity consumption is a general trend for all models except XGBoost. For those models, it is clear that predictions above 2000 kWh are commonly overestimated, while the errors for predictions under 2000 kWh are more evenly distributed around the perfect prediction line. This again points towards the effect of outliers on the performance of the models.
